Rose Blossoms is a nature-based rite-of-passage and mentorship program for adolescent girls. Through circle work, outdoor experiences, creative expression, stewardship projects, ritual, and community building, participants develop emotional resilience, deeper self-awareness, connection to the natural world, and supportive relationships with peers and mentors during the transition into young womanhood.

Rose Blossoms will gather seasonally during three weekends over the 2026-2027 school year in order to provide spacious time for deepening connections with ourselves, each other, and the earth, and the Rose Blossoms themes.

Parents and caregivers will be supported along the path and given tools to uplift their girls as they step into this process.

Between each weekend gathering, a Sunday afternoon family gathering will be held to support the ongoing program and build community within the group. These gatherings will be held on Sunday afternoons from 2-5pm. December 6, March 7, and the girls will be invited for sacred sharing at our seasonal final Base Camp sessions with the greater Wayfinders community.

How the journey unfolds

Program structure

Sessions: 2 seasonal camping trips in fall and spring (Orientation/First Gathering in Fall and Closing/Rite of Passage in Spring), plus Wintertime weekend sleepover, plus connecting gatherings between each weekend journey to keep the changes flowing, and a Summertime expo for the Wayfinders Community to share the learning and growth from the journey.

Group size: 12 participants to balance intimacy and diversity of perspectives.

Facilitators: Two trained adults (one lead, one assistant/guide) with experience in youth development, non-violent communication, outdoor education, girls rite of passage.

Session Rhythm: welcome circle → check-in → thematic exploration and/or skill practice → small-group project or reflection → closing ritual.

2026–2027 journey

Three seasons of connection and change.

October 23–25, 2026

Fall camping weekend

Mountain Lakes Park

Diving deep into ourselves, our history, our community, our past.

Girls will leave this weekend knowing themselves, each other, and the land so much better than when they arrived. Girls will have an opportunity to create an altar together which will illuminate the path they are on, as they call in support from a larger community. We will sleep under the stars alongside mountain lakes, and dream into the girls they are becoming. Parents/caregivers will be invited to send their daughters off in ceremony as they cross the threshold and begin this work, and invited to stay for a parent track session while the girls are off on their own journey.

January 8–10, 2027

Winter

Home in Westchester

Spa Queens—making, using, and gifting natural beauty products.

As the snow blankets the land, we will cozy up indoors and learn more about ourselves and our changing bodies. We will create and experience natural beauty care for ourselves and to gift to others on the journey.

May 7–9, 2027

Spring

Mountain Lakes or Upstate

Roses & Rivers—rose beads, paddle boarding, and river clean up.

Our final weekend together will bring forward all we have learned and experienced together as the girls authorize themselves to become who they are here to be. We will participate in a changing woman ceremony for them to share reflections of each other. Parents and caregivers will be invited to witness the girls as they cross over the threshold again as changed women.

A supported passage

Program details

Learn more about the care, community, and practices that shape Rose Blossoms.

Roles for Caregivers and Community

Caregivers support track provided for families.

Community members knowledgeable in relevant themes will join our circles.

Youth co-design: participants help plan themes, lead activities, and evaluate the program.

Outcomes and Evaluations

Short-term: improved emotional regulation, stronger peer connections, increased nature engagement & connection.

Medium-term: demonstrated leadership, reduced single-use waste and other sustainability practices.

Evaluation methods: participant reflections, caregiver feedback, facilitator observations, simple ecological metrics for projects.

Sustaining the Community

Create alumni circles for older youth to mentor newer cohorts.

Document activities and outcomes to support funding and partnerships.

Maintain seasonal traditions to anchor ongoing identity and belonging.

General Gathering Themes

Belonging — identity mapping, group agreements, trust-building activities.

Body & Mind — mindfulness, healthy boundaries, stress tools, moon cycles and synching.

Relationships — active listening, conflict resolution, consent education, non-violent communication.

Place — nature walks/hikes, local ecology, seasonal cycles.

Action — low-waste swaps, service projects, planting, clean ups, gifting.

Leadership & Celebration — project presentations, peer recognition, transition rituals.

Activities & Practices

Circle check-ins with emotion cards or journaling prompts.

Skill workshops: cooking, foraging, herbalism, self-care and beauty.

Creative projects: earth art, bead making & mala necklaces.

Stewardship actions: native planting, clean ups.

Reflection rituals: gratitude stones, intention-setting, closing songs.

Safety, Inclusion, and Accessibility

Co-created behavioral agreements and consent culture.

Deep mentorship and individualized learning goals.

Low-barrier participation: sliding-scale fees or scholarships; materials provided.
